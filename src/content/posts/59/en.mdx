Together with the Green Council Group, I filed a council initiative in Kirkkonummi on 11 May 2026 to strengthen the municipality's digital resilience. We propose that the municipality map its dependence on information systems outside Europe, prepare for service outages and move to open file formats for sharing files.

## Why does digital resilience concern municipalities?

The municipality relies on digital systems for nearly everything: remote meetings, archiving, email and contact between school and home. If a service goes down, a large part of the municipality's and its residents' daily life goes down with it. France, the Netherlands and Germany are already limiting their dependencies, and the services closest to residents come first in that work.

## What does the initiative propose?

The initiative has two parts. First, the municipality maps which systems and digital companies outside Europe it depends on and draws up contingency plans for a service becoming unavailable. Second, the municipality shares its files in open formats such as ODF and PDF/UA. A full break is not the goal: European services are introduced alongside the current ones.

## Where does the initiative stand?

The municipality is preparing a response to the initiative, and the council will consider it. I will update this post when the response is ready.

## The motion in full

*Filed with Kirkkonummi municipal council on 11 May 2026. Translated from the Finnish original.*

The municipality of Kirkkonummi relies on digital infrastructures in nearly all of its operations: from remote meeting and archiving services to email and contact between schools and homes. If digital services are not available, a large part of the activities of the municipality and its residents would be paralysed or at least significantly hampered. Changes in the global security environment have shown that digital independence must be given more attention than before.

Several European countries, including France, the Netherlands and Germany, are actively seeking to limit their digital dependencies. Starting the same work in Finland is also essential. Of paramount importance here are the services closest to residents and citizens, that is, among other things, the operations of municipalities.

Even though a full break from services outside Europe would not be realistic or even desirable, benefits can be achieved in the short and medium term by introducing European services alongside the current systems. In the long term, demand from Kirkkonummi and other Finnish municipalities for European service providers helps develop the European offering to become ever better.

Besides systems, another important aspect of digital resilience is data. At present, most files in the municipality are stored and distributed in closed file formats. This means that the municipality is dependent on the providers of these file formats in this respect too. Germany's federal government, for example, has responded to this by requiring file formats based on open standards for all files, either in line with the ODF standard or PDF/UA.

We, the undersigned councillors, propose that Kirkkonummi map its dependencies on information systems and digital companies outside Europe and draw up appropriate contingency plans for situations in which a service would not be available. In addition, Kirkkonummi will switch to using file formats based on open standards, such as ODF and PDF/UA, for sharing files.

Lauri Lavanti

The Green Council Group
