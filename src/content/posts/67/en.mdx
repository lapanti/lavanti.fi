I'm Lauri Lavanti, a Kirkkonummi municipal councillor and the Greens' parliamentary candidate in the Uusimaa electoral district for the 2027 election. I want Finland to secure its digital independence in the age of AI. I'm one of the founders of the [Digital Independence citizens' initiative](https://www.kansalaisaloite.fi/fi/aloite/16691), which gathered the required 50,000 signatures in July 2026 and will go before parliament in spring 2027. I want to build a Finland that uses AI and technology on its own terms, where no one gets left behind.

To me, politics means taking care of shared matters, sustainably and across party lines. Openness, honesty, and listening to different views matter, especially on hard questions.

## What expertise do I bring to parliament?

I hold an MSc from Aalto University and work as a lead developer, with more than 10 years of experience building secure software and banking services. I want to bring that practical expertise into parliament, so decisions actually serve everyday life for Finns.

Finland has spent decades building world-class technology, in companies, universities, and through their collaboration: from Vaisala to icebreakers, from Nokia to Linux and Supercell, to ICEYE and IQM. AI opens a new chapter in that story, but it requires parliament to understand what the technology actually demands in practice.

My campaign rests on three pillars: the economy, education, and freedom.

## Why does vendor dependency cost taxpayers?

I'm demanding that [public AI and digital procurement](/en/newsletter/8/when-a-vendor-cannot-be-switched/) be built open and portable, so vendor dependency doesn't drain tax money year after year. My example is eVaka, [the open-source early childhood education system developed jointly by Espoo, Tampere, Oulu, and Turku](https://www.sttinfo.fi/tiedote/70018983/nelja-kaupunkia-kehitti-yhteisen-varhaiskasvatuksen-tietojarjestelman-nyt-myos-muut-kunnat-voivat-ottaa-sen-kayttoonsa). More than ten municipalities already benefit from it, and because the costs are shared, it saves money every year. The same principle has to apply to AI procurement: openness and portability need to be procurement conditions from day one.

## How does education stay equal in the age of AI?

Skills need to [stay current throughout life](/en/blog/27/is-education-under-special-protection/) and be equally available to everyone, from vocational training to higher education. I'm deeply concerned about the funding cuts to both [vocational](https://www.sivista.fi/blogi/ammatillisen-koulutuksen-leikkaukset-rakeita-taivaan-taydelta/) and [university education](https://unifi.fi/puolivaliriihi-tiedote/) — diverse expertise is the foundation of Finland's competitiveness.

I want to highlight the role of libraries in particular: they're one of the few places that offer free access to knowledge and skills without harvesting user data. In the age of AI, that makes them essential guardians of equality.

## How does AI threaten fundamental rights?

AI makes mass surveillance technically easier, which is exactly why legislation has to hold the line on fundamental rights. I've written before about [why the European Parliament was right to reject the chat control proposal](/en/blog/54/chat-control-parliament-was-right/): the same principle applies to any automatic scanning of private messages.

Without [strong privacy protection](/en/blog/45/fundamental-rights-must-not-be-dismantled-without-justification/), democracy cannot function, and AI must never be used to weaken it. Fundamental rights have to be the starting point for all legislation, not a variable.

## What does digital independence mean?

The economy, education, and freedom come together in my campaign as one central goal: digital independence. That doesn't mean isolation — it means building genuine domestic and European alternatives for when we need them. I've written before about [why digital independence is a necessity](/en/blog/51/digital-independence-is-a-necessity/) and [how it's built one step at a time](/en/blog/53/digital-independence-is-built-one-step-at-a-time/).

My case is backed by solid research: according to a [survey published by Sitra and the Finnish Business and Policy Forum (EVA) in May 2026](https://www.sitra.fi/uutiset/kysely-turvaa-ja-toimintakykya-suomalaiset-haluavat-vahvan-ja-omavaraisen-euroopan-unionin/), 77 percent of Finns believe Europe needs to reduce its technological and economic dependence on the United States, even while still viewing it as an important security partner. That widespread concern now calls for concrete action.

My vision is clear: in the age of AI, Finland can see new companies and new jobs emerge, while everyone's freedom stays guaranteed even as technology develops fast, and no one gets left behind.
