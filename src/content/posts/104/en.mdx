This week Finland celebrates Independence Day, and it is a good moment to stop and think about what independence actually means to us.

## What does independence mean to us?

To me, independence means above all that we get to decide our own affairs. That is not something to take for granted. Earlier generations worked enormously hard so that we would have a country of our own and working services we can rely on. Finland has also always known how to prepare for bad days. We keep [emergency stockpiles of medicine, oil and grain](/en/blog/82/security-of-supply-ends-at-the-cloud/), and they were built up in good times, not only once the crisis had already arrived. A big thank you belongs to those who built this country before us.

More and more of everyday life now happens online. Messages from a child's school arrive in an app, and prescriptions are renewed on the web. Yet we rarely stop to think whose systems all of this runs on, and what happens if one of them stops working.

That is why I hope Finland will in future be [independent in the digital world as well](/en/blog/85/digital-independence-happy-hacking-day/). It does not mean isolation, nor that we should build everything ourselves. But I do hope that the systems our everyday life depends on are ones we can fix or replace when something breaks. I hope that [the data about us stays in our own hands](/en/blog/79/finland-risks-losing-control-of-its-own-data/).

Just as earlier generations made sure there was enough food and medicine in hard times, we can make sure our digital services keep working even when the world is in turmoil. It is entirely doable, but it will not happen by itself.

Happy Independence Day, everyone! Let's take care of each other and of this country, online too.
