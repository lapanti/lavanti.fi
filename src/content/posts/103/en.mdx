I gave the Green council group's speech at the Kirkkonummi municipal council on 16 November 2026, when the council was handling the Kirkkonummi budget for 2027. Our group did not support the budget, because about €700,000 in total is cut from Finnish- and Swedish-language early childhood education and care without a child impact assessment. The council approved the budget during Children's Rights Week.

On this page you will find the council's decision, the speech as a video and the whole speech as I delivered it. In the speech I go through what we know about the municipality's finances and population growth, and why services should be decided only once their effects are known.

## What did the council decide on the Kirkkonummi budget for 2027?

On 16 November 2026 the Kirkkonummi municipal council approved the 2027 budget and the financial plan for 2028–2029, as the municipality reports in its [press release](https://kirkkonummi.fi/kunnanvaltuusto-hyvaksyi-vuoden-2027-talousarvion-ja-vuosien-2028-2029-taloussuunnitelman/) (in Finnish). The council also approved a guiding statement proposed by Saara Huhmarniemi of the Greens. According to the statement, decisions affecting personnel are to be planned proactively and with a long-term view as the municipality adjusts its finances. In addition, Kirkkonummi's reputation as a good employer is safeguarded. According to the original estimate, the budget cuts €500,000 from Finnish-language and €200,000 from Swedish-language early childhood education.

At the meeting we proposed cancelling these cuts, but our proposal did not get enough support.

## Why did the Green council group not support the budget?

The Green council group did not support the Kirkkonummi budget because it cuts about €700,000 from early childhood education. The impact on children was not assessed. Good governance means assessing the effects of a decision before it is made. In the spring we will update the service network plan, and that is the right place to assess the volume and quality of services.

## The speech in full

*Delivered at the Kirkkonummi municipal council on 16 November 2026.*

*Translation of the Finnish original.*

Honourable Chair, ärade ordförande\
Dear councillors, bästa fullmäktigeledamöter\
Happy Children's Rights Week, trevlig barnrättsvecka

The timing is fitting, because today we are deciding on Kirkkonummi's budget for next year, which directly affects services for children. Before the figures, though, it is worth pausing for a moment on why we make these decisions at all.

The municipality's fundamental task is to provide services for its residents. The council steers and oversees that work. As is natural in a democracy, we have different opinions and emphases here. At bottom, however, I believe we all share a common goal: to make sure that Kirkkonummi remains a vital municipality that is a good place to live.

We reach that goal only when we base our decisions on the best available information. So let us recap what we know.

We know that the municipality's financial situation is serious, and that it will weaken towards the 2030s if we continue on the current course. Scenario calculations by the municipal officials show that without significant changes the deficit would grow to over five million euros a year in the 2030s.

We also know that population growth is vital for the municipality's finances. Every year the working-age population does not grow, our financial situation is almost a million euros tighter.

We also know that people moving to Kirkkonummi come mainly from Espoo and Helsinki. Income taxation in both is considerably lighter than ours, so the reason for moving most likely lies in the environment and the services. Services are indeed one of our municipality's most important attractions. Those services are the concrete matter we are deciding on today. We are deciding whether a familiar and safe adult greets a child at daycare in the morning, and whether a child is allowed to draw and craft to their heart's content. We are deciding on things whose effects will be visible for the rest of the children's lives.

Customarily, the municipal board decides in the spring on a framework within which the committees plan their budgets for the following year. This year the framework was already tight: it included a savings target of about €1.7 million for the sectors. That too was achieved through good preparation.

On top of this, however, the budget negotiations ended in a targeted cut to the core of residents' everyday services, early childhood education. According to the original estimate, €500,000 is cut from Finnish-language early childhood education and €200,000 from Swedish-language. The impact on children was not assessed.

This is anything but responsible financial management or good governance. Representatives of the municipality's staff have asked the council to assess the effects on personnel carefully before decisions are made. That has not been done now.

We in the Green council group carry our responsibility for developing the municipality and for sustainable municipal finances. Last year the council approved a programme for sustainable municipal finances, which agreed the means and guidelines for correcting the situation. We are committed to that, because decisions made without careful preparation do not stand on firm ground.

In the spring we will update the service network plan. That is the right place to assess the volume and quality of services with a long-term view, and to make carefully prepared and considered decisions on services.

The outcome of the budget negotiations does not follow the lines agreed earlier. To crown it all, it makes children and young people carry the responsibility on our behalf. That is why we do not support the proposal for the municipal budget.

I ask every councillor to consider carefully today. Do we want to safeguard the municipality's services and vitality, now and in the future? Or do we accept a cut to children's everyday lives without knowing its effects?

Let us make a decision during Children's Rights Week that keeps Kirkkonummi a vital and good place to live.

Thank you. Tack.
